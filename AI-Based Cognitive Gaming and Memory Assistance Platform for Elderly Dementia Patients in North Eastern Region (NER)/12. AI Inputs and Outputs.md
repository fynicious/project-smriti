
# 28. What AI Inputs Can Be Used?

AI is only as good as the data it receives. For a dementia support platform, the most valuable inputs are **digital biomarkers**—behavioral signals collected continuously from games, daily activities, reminders, speech, and optional wearable devices. Modern research shows that combining multiple data sources produces better prediction of cognitive decline than relying on a single memory test. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38276235/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 1. Cognitive Game Performance (Most Important)

Every game played by the patient becomes a cognitive assessment.

Collect:

- Game score
- Accuracy (% correct)
- Number of mistakes
- Number of skipped questions
- Completion rate
- Time taken per question
- Overall completion time
- Consecutive correct answers
- Consecutive incorrect answers
- Difficulty level reached
- Improvement compared to previous sessions

Example:

Patient normally scores **92%**.

Over several weeks:

92% → 88% → 80% → 72%

The AI detects a downward trend before it becomes obvious to caregivers.

---

## 2. Response Speed

Reaction time is one of the earliest indicators of cognitive change.

Measure:

- Average response time
- Fastest response
- Slowest response
- Delay before starting a game
- Time spent thinking
- Response consistency

Example:

Earlier:

Average response = **2.4 s**

Later:

Average response = **5.8 s**

Even if accuracy stays high, increasing response time may indicate slowing cognitive processing. Response latency is considered a valuable digital biomarker in modern cognitive assessment systems. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13169892/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

## 3. Memory Performance

Track memory-specific behaviors.

Features:

- Immediate recall score
- Delayed recall score
- Recognition accuracy
- Repeated mistakes
- Forgotten objects
- Forgotten sequences
- Story recall accuracy
- Picture recall accuracy

The AI should compare performance against the patient's own baseline instead of only comparing with other users.

---

## 4. Executive Function Metrics

Many dementia patients develop planning and decision-making difficulties.

Measure:

- Puzzle completion time
- Planning efficiency
- Number of unnecessary moves
- Ability to follow multi-step instructions
- Task switching speed
- Logical reasoning accuracy

---

## 5. Attention Metrics

Attention often declines before severe memory impairment.

Useful features:

- Focus duration
- Missed targets
- Incorrect taps
- Distracted pauses
- Sustained attention score
- Selective attention score

---

## 6. Hint Usage

Hints are a strong indicator of increasing dependence.

Collect:

- Number of hints
- Hint frequency
- Hint type
- Questions requiring hints
- Trend over time

Example:

Week 1:

2 hints/day

Week 8:

15 hints/day

This suggests increasing difficulty despite unchanged game content.

---

## 7. Voice Interaction

Voice provides valuable behavioral information beyond spoken words.

Track:

- Voice assistant usage frequency
- Speech duration
- Speaking speed
- Long pauses
- Repeated questions
- Command completion
- Failed voice commands
- Speech clarity (if technically feasible)
- Vocabulary richness (with appropriate privacy controls)

Research increasingly shows that speech-based features can support early cognitive assessment. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13169892/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

## 8. Reminder Compliance

Medication adherence is critical.

Monitor:

- Medicine taken on time
- Missed medicines
- Delayed medicines
- Water reminders completed
- Exercise reminders completed
- Appointment reminders acknowledged
- Reminder dismissal frequency

Example:

Patient ignores 80% of medication reminders this week.

The AI flags declining adherence.

---

## 9. Daily App Usage

Engagement itself is an important health signal.

Measure:

- Daily login
- Session duration
- Games completed
- Days skipped
- Time of day used
- Weekly activity trend

A sudden reduction in engagement may indicate worsening cognition, illness, depression, or loss of motivation.

---

## 10. Mood Logs

Mood affects cognition and quality of life.

Collect:

- Daily mood
- Anxiety level
- Stress level
- Happiness
- Irritability
- Depression screening (optional)
- Mood changes over time

Persistent low mood may warrant caregiver attention.

---

## 11. Sleep Data (Wearable Integration)

Sleep disturbances are strongly associated with cognitive decline.

Possible inputs:

- Sleep duration
- Deep sleep
- REM sleep
- Night awakenings
- Sleep efficiency
- Bedtime consistency

Wearable-derived sleep and circadian rhythm patterns are emerging digital biomarkers for dementia risk assessment. [JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2026/1/e86262/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

## 12. Physical Activity (Optional)

Physical activity is associated with brain health.

Collect:

- Daily steps
- Walking duration
- Walking speed
- Distance travelled
- Sedentary time
- Exercise frequency

A sudden reduction in mobility may indicate worsening health or cognition.

---

## 13. Caregiver Observations

Not all information can be collected automatically.

Allow caregivers to record:

- Confusion episodes
- Wandering incidents
- Falls
- Agitation
- Appetite
- Sleep quality
- Behavioral changes
- Daily functioning

These observations improve AI predictions.

---

## 14. Clinical Data (Optional)

If integrated with healthcare providers:

- MMSE score
- MoCA score
- Diagnosis stage
- Comorbidities
- Medication list
- Blood pressure
- Diabetes status
- Vision/hearing issues

Clinical information complements digital data rather than replacing it. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38276235/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

## Best AI Input Set

|Category|Examples|
|---|---|
|Cognitive|Scores, accuracy, recall, attention|

|Health|Medication adherence, hydration|

# 29. What Outputs Should AI Produce?

The AI should not only predict decline—it should generate **actionable recommendations** for patients, caregivers, and clinicians. Explainable outputs are especially important in healthcare so users understand _why_ the AI reached a conclusion. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38276235/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

## 1. Cognitive Risk Score

A single score estimating current cognitive risk.

Example:

```
Risk Score

0–30   Low Risk
31–60  Moderate Risk
61–100 High Risk
```

The score should combine:

- Memory performance
- Attention
- Reaction time
- Adherence
- Sleep
- Mood
- Activity

Also display confidence or uncertainty rather than only a single number.

---

## 2. Cognitive Trend Analysis

Rather than isolated scores, visualize progress.

Example:

```
January     Stable

February    Slight improvement

March       Stable

April       Mild decline

May         Significant decline
```

Charts make long-term changes easier to understand.

---

## 3. Personalized Difficulty Adjustment

Automatically adapt exercises.

If performance remains high:

- Increase difficulty
- Introduce new tasks
- Reduce hints

If performance falls:

- Simplify games
- Increase hints
- Repeat important exercises
- Slow progression

This keeps users engaged without frustration.

---

## 4. Personalized Game Recommendations

Recommend activities targeting weaker cognitive domains.

Examples:

Poor memory →

- Recall games
- Story memory
- Image recall

Poor attention →

- Focus games
- Reaction games

Poor executive function →

- Sequencing
- Planning puzzles

This creates individualized cognitive training rather than one-size-fits-all exercises.

---

## 5. Daily Recommendations

Provide simple guidance.

Examples:

- Play one memory game today
- Complete attention training
- Drink more water
- Walk for 20 minutes
- Sleep before 10 PM
- Practice voice exercises

---

## 6. Medication Risk Prediction

Estimate likelihood of missed medication.

Example:

```
Today's adherence probability

96%
```

If risk is high:

- Send reminders
- Notify caregivers (with consent)
- Escalate persistent issues

---

## 7. Caregiver Alerts

Only trigger meaningful alerts to reduce alarm fatigue.

Examples:

- Missed medication
- Significant cognitive decline
- Multiple skipped sessions
- Fall detected (if supported)
- Confusion episode
- Poor sleep for several nights
- Rapid behavioral changes

Include the reason behind every alert.

---

## 8. Weekly and Monthly Reports

Summaries for caregivers and clinicians.

Include:

- Memory score trend
- Attention trend
- Mood trend
- Sleep trend
- Medication adherence
- Activity level
- Game completion
- AI recommendations
- Areas of improvement
- Areas needing attention

---

## 9. Engagement Prediction

Estimate the likelihood of future app use.

Example:

```
Engagement Prediction

High

85% probability of completing tomorrow's session
```

If engagement is expected to drop:

- Recommend easier games
- Add motivational messages
- Suggest caregiver encouragement

---

## 10. Early Decline Detection

The AI should detect gradual deterioration before it becomes clinically obvious.

Example:

> "Reaction time has increased by 28% over the last six weeks while memory accuracy has decreased by 12%. Consider a clinical cognitive assessment."

The goal is to support earlier intervention, not to make a diagnosis.

---

## 11. Explainable AI Insights

Instead of displaying only predictions, explain them.

Example:

```
Risk increased because:

✓ Memory accuracy decreased
✓ Reaction time slowed
✓ Medication adherence dropped
✓ Sleep quality worsened
```

Explainable AI improves trust and supports clinician decision-making. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38276235/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

---

## 12. Clinician Dashboard Summary

Generate concise clinical summaries such as:

- Estimated cognitive trajectory
- High-risk domains
- Functional changes
- Adherence trends
- Suggested interventions
- Recommended follow-up timing

This reduces the time clinicians spend reviewing raw data.

## Best Features for Your NER Dementia Project

For a practical, research-backed system, prioritize:

1. **Game performance** (accuracy, score, errors)
2. **Reaction time and response latency**
3. **Longitudinal cognitive trends**
4. **Medication adherence**
5. **Mood tracking**
6. **Voice interaction metrics**
7. **Sleep and activity** (when wearables are available)
8. **Caregiver observations**
9. **Personalized game recommendations**
10. **Explainable risk scoring with clear reasons**
11. **Adaptive difficulty based on patient progress**
12. **Automated weekly reports for caregivers and clinicians**

This combination aligns well with current evidence that continuous, multimodal digital biomarkers and personalized AI provide more informative monitoring than periodic cognitive tests alone.
