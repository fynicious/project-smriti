
The three users have completely different goals:

|User|Primary Goal|Needs|
|---|---|---|
|**Patient**|Complete daily activities|Simple UI, games, reminders, encouragement|
|**Caregiver (Family)**|Monitor and assist|Daily progress, alerts, medicine adherence, safety|
|**Healthcare Worker / Doctor**|Clinical decision making|Trends, risk prediction, cognitive decline, reports|

This separation follows current digital dementia care research, where patient-facing applications are paired with caregiver dashboards and clinician decision-support systems rather than one universal interface. # 21. Why Three Separate Dashboards?

Different users require different information.

A dementia patient should **not** see complex medical charts.

A caregiver needs continuous monitoring and alerts.

A healthcare professional requires summarized clinical insights instead of minute-by-minute activity.

Separating dashboards makes the system:

- Easier to use
- Less confusing
- More secure
- Better suited for each user's responsibilities

This user-centered approach is widely adopted in modern dementia care platforms. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12401067/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
